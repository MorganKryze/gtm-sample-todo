# Due dates: spec

> Track: Feature. Date: 2026-10-28. Mockups: none.
> Brainstorm: .gtm/brainstorms/2026-10-28-next-feature.md.
> Status: approved by the human on 2026-10-28 (delegated: "fais le max en autonomie"); plan kept from the one pass

## Summary
Une todo peut porter une échéance : `todo add <texte> --due AAAA-MM-JJ`. `list` affiche la date
après le texte et écrit `overdue` sur une todo ouverte dont l'échéance est passée.
Pourquoi maintenant : c'est l'idée retenue du brainstorm, et elle tient en une branche courte.
Garde-fous : un ancien `todo.json` sans échéance se charge tel quel ; une date invalide est
refusée, jamais stockée ; le fuseau horaire ne décale jamais une date.
À trancher : deux cartes ci-dessous. Je recommande A pour les deux : « en retard » se juge sur la
date du calendrier local (une todo due aujourd'hui n'est pas en retard), et `list` garde l'ordre
des numéros.
Estimation : no measured run yet.

## What and why
A person can give a todo a due date when adding it, and `list` shows that date and points out the
open todos whose date has passed. Today the list says what to do but never by when, so a todo that
mattered on Monday looks the same on Friday. The date is a calendar day, not a moment: the person
typed a day, and that day is what they get back, on any machine, at any hour.

Success looks like: a person adds `buy milk --due 2026-11-02`, and on 2026-11-03 `todo list` shows
that line with the date and the word `overdue`, while every todo written before this feature lists
exactly as before.

## Out of scope
- Changing or removing the due date of an existing todo (a later `todo due <id>` story).
- Sorting or filtering by due date (`list --open`, sorting): dropped in the brainstorm; card 2 keeps
  id order.
- Times of day, relative dates (`tomorrow`, `+3d`), reminders or notifications.
- An atomic save: a separate story from the brainstorm's dropped ideas; this feature does not
  change how the file is written.

## Acceptance (EARS)
Each line is testable and becomes at least one test.
- A1 WHEN the person runs `todo add <text> --due YYYY-MM-DD` with a real calendar day THE SYSTEM
  SHALL store the todo with that due date, character for character as typed.
- A2 WHEN the person runs `todo add <text>` without `--due` THE SYSTEM SHALL store the todo with
  no due date, in the same shape as before this feature.
- A3 IF the due date is not a real calendar day written YYYY-MM-DD (for example `2026-13-01`,
  `2026-02-30`, `2026-2-3`, `02/11/2026`) THEN THE SYSTEM SHALL print one `todo: <message>` line
  naming the expected form, exit with code 1, and leave the list file byte for byte unchanged.
- A4 IF `--due` is given with no date after it THEN THE SYSTEM SHALL print one `todo: <message>`
  line, exit with code 1, and leave the list file byte for byte unchanged.
- A5 WHEN the list file holds todos without a due date (a file written before this feature) THE
  SYSTEM SHALL load, list, complete and save them as before, never adding a field to them.
- A6 WHEN the person runs `todo list` THE SYSTEM SHALL show each dated todo's due date after its
  text, and show an undated todo exactly as before.
- A7 WHILE an open todo's due date is before today's local calendar date THE SYSTEM SHALL mark its
  line with the word `overdue`.
- A8 THE SYSTEM SHALL NOT mark a todo `overdue` when it is done, or when its due date is today or
  later.
- A9 THE SYSTEM SHALL take today as the local calendar date of the machine, the same at 00:01 and
  at 23:59, and SHALL never shift a stored due date by the machine's time zone.
- A10 THE SYSTEM SHALL keep `todo list` in id order, dated or not.

## Decisions
| # | Decision | Choice | Why | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | On what date is "overdue" judged? | A: the local calendar date, a todo due today is not overdue | the person thinks in days on their own clock (card 1) | approved (delegated, A) |
| D2 | Does `list` sort by due date? | A: keep id order, mark overdue only | ids stay where the person last saw them; the smallest change (card 2) | approved (delegated, A) |
| D3 | How is "overdue" shown? | the word `overdue` on the line, no symbol or colour | the brief: meaning never by symbol alone beyond `[ ]`/`[x]`; a screen reader reads it | ruling |
| D4 | Is a done todo ever overdue? | no | it is finished; marking it would be noise | ruling |
| D5 | What does the file keep? | the date string as typed; an undated todo carries no due field at all | a calendar day has no time zone to lose; old and undated todos stay byte-identical (lens: First Principles Analysis) | ruling |
| D6 | What counts as a valid date? | a real day in YYYY-MM-DD only; no rollover (`2026-02-30` is refused, not read as March 2) | a bad date stored silently is the brainstorm's first failure (lens: Inversion Analysis) | ruling |

Open decision cards (one pass: the plan follows each recommendation):

**Décision 1 : sur quelle date juge-t-on qu'une todo est en retard ?**

| Option | Ce que ça veut dire |
| --- | --- |
| **A** Date du calendrier local | aujourd'hui = la date de la machine ; en retard dès le lendemain de l'échéance, à toute heure |
| **B** Date UTC | aujourd'hui = la date UTC ; à Paris après minuit, ou à New York le soir, « aujourd'hui » n'est pas le jour de la personne |

**Je recommande : A**, la personne a tapé un jour de son calendrier, c'est ce jour-là qui compte ; B recrée exactement le décalage de fuseau que le brainstorm redoute (lens: Architecture Decision Records). **Coût si c'est faux :** une todo marquée en retard un jour trop tôt ou trop tard ; corriger, c'est changer une seule fonction.

**Décision 2 : `list` trie-t-elle par échéance ?**

| Option | Ce que ça veut dire |
| --- | --- |
| **A** Ordre des numéros, marque seulement | les lignes restent où elles étaient, `overdue` se lit sur la ligne |
| **B** Tri par échéance | les todos datées d'abord, la plus proche en tête, puis les autres |

**Je recommande : A**, `done <id>` se lit à côté de la liste et un ordre stable évite de chercher son numéro ; le tri peut venir avec `list --open`, dans sa propre story (lens: Architecture Decision Records). **Coût si c'est faux :** une petite story de plus pour trier, sans toucher au fichier.

## Data, security, accessibility
- Data: each todo may gain an optional due date, a calendar day kept as text. An older file
  without it still loads (data.md: a new field is optional on read). No migration, no removed
  field, ids untouched. A refused date writes nothing.
- Security: one more argv input, validated before anything is written; no new trust boundary.
- Accessibility: plain text; `overdue` is a word, read in order after the date.

## Open questions
None. The brainstorm's two questions are cards 1 and 2 above; every other choice is a ruling in
